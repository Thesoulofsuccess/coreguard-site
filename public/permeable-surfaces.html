Coreguard/Permeable surfaces

Let the ground breathe.Surfaces that work with water.

A properly designed permeable surface lets rain pass through, holds it in a purpose-built aggregate sub-base and, where the ground allows, lets it soak back in.

01How it works

Through, not across.Switch between surface types.

{{pmNote}}

1 · The surfaceResin-bound aggregate, permeable paving with open joints, or pervious concrete. Water passes through it rather than across it.

2 · Bedding or laying courseA free-draining layer that the surface is laid on, sized so fine particles do not wash down.

3 · Open-graded sub-baseClean, coarse aggregate with voids between the stones. It carries the load and holds water.

4 · Temporary storageDuring heavy rain the voids fill, then release water slowly, into the ground or to an outlet.

5 · Sub-grade, and outlet where neededTested soil decides the type: full infiltration, partial infiltration with an outlet, or a lined system with no infiltration.

02Suitability

Eight things that decide it.Assessed on every site.

Not every surface should be permeable, and not every permeable surface can let water soak in.

01Soil permeability

Tested on site. It decides whether water can soak in, partly or not at all.

02Groundwater level

A high water table, or contaminated ground, means a lined system instead of infiltration.

03Slope

Steeper sites need the sub-base designed so water does not simply run downhill inside it.

04Traffic loading

Footpaths, driveways and parking need different surface and sub-base build-ups.

05Sediment & clogging

Soil, sand and debris washed on from nearby areas are the main cause of clogging.

06Maintenance

Regular sweeping or vacuum cleaning. Never sealed over, never sanded, never pressure-washed.

07Local drainage rules

Outlets and discharge rates follow local requirements and the site's drainage design.

08Right place

Not every surface should be permeable. Fuel areas and heavily soiled sites are usually lined or avoided.

The idea

A surface can be beautiful. It can protect. And in the right place, it can let rain return to the earth.

Infiltration depends on site conditions, design and maintenance. Coreguard assesses each site before recommending a permeable system, and will say so when one is not suitable.
03Materials & technology

The system for the structure.Never the other way round.

Many surfaces look alike and behave very differently. A resin floor, a resin-bonded path and a resin-bound path can all be called "resin", but only one of them lets water through.
